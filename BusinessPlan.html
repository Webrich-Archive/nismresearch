Section 01 — Market

The NISM certification market is large, regulated, and growing.

100,000+ candidates/year attempt NISM certifications — mandatory for anyone selling, advising, or managing securities in India.
50–60% national pass rate. Nearly half fail on first attempt and re-take at their own cost (₹1,500–2,500 per exam).
85–90% pass rate for candidates who use structured prep — a 30+ percentage-point lift.
₹280 trillion Indian financial services sector requires these certifications as the licensing backbone.
Career-path pressure: a single candidate often takes 3–5 exams across their career (V-A → V-B → X-A → XV). Per-exam pricing penalises them.
Candidate pain points (Quora / Reddit / LinkedIn)
  1. Workbook sample questions are too easy → false confidence → fail by 1–5 marks.
  2. "NISM books are not lucid" — language is confusing.
  3. Outdated notes from 2024 still circulating in the market.
  4. Theoretical concepts are the weak spot; numericals are easier.
  5. Recent exams are "tricky and conceptual" — rote mock banks don't prepare candidates.
  6. Students piece together workbook + YouTube + mocks + Telegram — no single home.
  7. No one offers career-path bundles at a sensible price.

Section 03 — Our Edge

Three structural advantages the market cannot easily replicate.

01

Breadth

Every NISM exam — including niche and new ones like XXIII Social Impact Assessor (Jan 2026) and XIX-E Cat III AIF Managers. Competitors cap out at 26–28 series. Taking a candidate from V-A through X-B through XV keeps them inside our product for their entire career.

02

Depth

Every question carries a 4-section explanation: Logic (why it's right), Trap (why the distractors tempt), Cross-Exam Context (how this concept recurs in other exams), and Pro-Tip (execution-level insight from a practitioner). Competitors offer 3–5 generic sentences.

03

Truth

Difficulty is tuned to the actual current exam — Moderate-to-Hard — not the easy workbook samples that breed false confidence. This is the most common student complaint; we solve it at the content layer, not with marketing.

Example of our 4-section explanation
"Which SAS standard applies when an SE runs a rural ANC programme funded via a ZCZP bond?"
The Logic
SAS 200 governs healthcare, mental healthcare, sanitation, and safe drinking water. ANC check-ups fall squarely under healthcare service delivery…
The Trap
Candidates pick SAS 400 (gender) because ANC targets women. But SAS 400 is about structural gender equality, not maternal health delivery…
Cross-Exam Context
X-A / X-B advisers recommend SSE-listed ZCZP bonds to donor HNIs for Section 80G benefit — this concept recurs in the adviser track…
Assessor's Pro-Tip
Triangulate hospital records + ANC register + village health worker log. Never accept a single source for a beneficiary count over 1,000…

Architecture

Supabase as the unified identity and data layer

  • Auth: magic link + Google + Apple, SDK-native on all three clients.
  • Postgres: exams, topics, questions, attempts, subscriptions — row-level security for direct client reads.
  • Payments: Razorpay (UPI + cards + net banking) for India; Stripe layered on later.
  • Offline: SQLite cache of purchased exam content; attempts sync on reconnect.
  • Cost: Supabase free tier covers 50K MAU; total infra < ₹15K/month at 10K DAU.
Why candidates stay

Retention engine, not just a mock-test bank

  • Spaced repetition of missed questions — proven memory protocol.
  • Cross-exam recommender — "You cleared V-A; here's why X-A is your natural next step." LTV multiplier.
  • Readiness score — topic-weighted, probabilistic pass-likelihood; transparent improvement plan.
  • AI tutor (Phase 3) — RAG over the 4-section explanation corpus; chat-based doubt resolution.
  • Pass guarantee — refund if first-attempt score < 60 after N practice hours. Risk-reversal marketing.

Section 08 — Risks & mitigations

Honest view of what could go wrong.

Content drift

Risk: SEBI updates syllabus, our bank goes stale. Mitigation: version-controlled Git repo with monthly refresh cadence; automated SEBI circular monitoring (same pipeline we already run).

Price race to the bottom

Risk: incumbent drops to ₹99/mo. Mitigation: compete on depth (4-section explanations) and catalogue breadth, not price. B2B margin insulates B2C.

Sales cycle on B2B

Risk: enterprise HR/L&D moves slowly. Mitigation: free pilot (25 seats, 30 days) lowers activation friction; B2C revenue sustains the business while B2B matures.

Platform dependency

Risk: Supabase / Vercel pricing change. Mitigation: Postgres + open-source SDKs → portable to any provider with 1–2 weeks of migration work.
